# Waiting Periods

## The three clocks
```text
Policy starts
│
├── Day 0–30     Initial wait: only accidents are covered
├── Month 24*    Specified diseases (hernia, cataract, joints, ...) become covered
└── Month 36*    Pre-existing diseases (PED) become covered
                 (* typical defaults; IRDAI caps PED and specified waits at 3 years)
```
- A condition can sit on **both** clocks. Example: a thyroid disorder that is also on the plan's specified-disease list. **The longer wait usually applies.**
- So cutting only the PED wait may not help that condition. You'd need both reductions, and some plans allow the specified-disease cut only below a certain age (e.g. 45).
- Complications of a PED (e.g. diabetes → kidney, eye or heart problems) may be treated as part of the PED. Ask.

## Ways to shorten them
| Option | What it does | Typical cost pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **PED reduction add-on** (e.g. 36 → 24 or 12 months) | Brings the PED clock forward | % loading on that person's base premium, paid **only for the first 2–3 years** |
| **Specified-disease reduction** | Same for the specified list | Similar; often has an age limit |
| **Day-1 / day-31 chronic riders** | Cover named conditions (usually diabetes, BP, asthma, cholesterol) almost immediately | Costly; each rider covers only its listed conditions |
| **Plans with a shorter default** | Some plans have 24 months (or less) built in | Higher base price, nothing extra to buy |
| **Portability credit** | Time served on a previous individual policy counts | Free, but limited to the old sum insured |

### How to think about the cost
A reduction is a **one-time price for earlier cover**, not a permanent premium increase. Work it out:

```text
loading % × that person's base premium × number of years it is charged
= total extra paid for N months of earlier cover
```
Example (made-up numbers): 40% loading on a ₹12,000 base for 2 years = ₹9,600 to be covered 24 months earlier.

## When a reduction is NOT worth it
If another policy you hold already covers the condition during the wait, you're paying twice. Take the reduction anyway (or a mid-way one, like 36 → 24) if:
- that other cover might end before the wait is over,
- its sum insured is small or shared, or
- its terms can change every year.

A middle option often gives the best value: cheaper per month saved and it caps the worst case.

## Other wait-related traps
- **Increasing the sum insured later** usually restarts the waits **on the extra amount only**.
- **Missing a renewal** restarts everything. Renew before the due date.
- Moratorium (5 years) is separate: it protects against non-disclosure disputes, it doesn't shorten waits.
