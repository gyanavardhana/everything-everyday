# Disclosure and Underwriting

## The #1 cause of rejected claims: non-disclosure
The pattern seen again and again in consumer-court cases:
```text
Hospital admission → cashless denied → claim rejected as "pre-existing, not disclosed"
→ consumer court reverses it 1–3 years later (if you fight)
```
- **Telling the agent is not disclosure.** Only what's written on the signed proposal form counts.
- Fill in the form **yourself**, or check every answer before signing. Ask for a copy of the completed form **before** paying.
- Some forms have specific yes/no boxes (diabetes, BP, thyroid, joints…). Others have one generic "any illness?" box. With a generic box, **write each condition out in full**.
- Declare old surgeries, current symptoms and **every existing insurance policy**.
- Insurers share past-claims data through the Insurance Information Bureau (IIB). Assume they can see previous hospital claims.

## Missing medical records (e.g. an old surgery)
Not having records is not a reason to hide it. Do this instead:
1. Declare it on the form: what was done, roughly when, and that records aren't available.
2. Try to get them from the hospital's records department (discharge summary, operation notes). Even a letter saying they're unavailable helps.
3. Get a short **current note from a specialist** describing the present condition.
4. Accept a **condition-specific exclusion** if offered (e.g. one joint only). That's far better than a rejected claim on everything later.

## Underwriting: what happens after you apply
| Step | What to know |
|---|---|
| Medical tests | Insurer decides by age, sum insured and conditions. Some pay for all tests; others refund ≥50% if the policy is issued |
| Key numbers | For diabetes, **HbA1c** is the main number. No insurer publishes a fixed cut-off; your actual value decides loading or acceptance |
| Decision | Accept as is, accept with **loading**, accept with an **exclusion**, or decline |
| Loading | Can be per condition and per person (caps like 100–200% exist). Usually **continues at every renewal**. Ask if it can be reviewed if health improves |
| Add-ons | Underwriting can refuse add-ons (like wait reductions) because of your conditions |

## Ask for the decision in writing **before** paying
For each person: conditions accepted, exclusions, loading % per condition. Brochure prices mean little until you have this.
