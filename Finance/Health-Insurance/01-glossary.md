# Glossary

| Term | Meaning | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Sum insured (SI)** | The most the policy pays in a policy year | Pick it for one big hospital stay in your city, plus inflation |
| **Family floater** | One sum insured shared by everyone on the policy | Cheaper, but one big claim uses it up for all. Usually priced on the **eldest** member's age |
| **Individual policy** | Separate sum insured per person | Costs more; no sharing |
| **Premium** | What you pay per year | Rises with age bands (often every 5 years) and with loading |
| **PED (pre-existing disease)** | Any condition diagnosed or treated in the 36 months before the policy starts | Covered only after the PED waiting period |
| **Initial waiting period** | Usually 30 days after the first policy starts; only accidents are covered | Not repeated at renewal |
| **Specified-disease waiting period** | A list of conditions (hernia, cataract, joints, ENT, thyroid in some plans…) with their own wait, often 24 months | A condition can be both a PED and on this list; usually **the longer wait applies** |
| **Moratorium period** | After 5 continuous years of cover, the insurer can't reject a claim for non-disclosure unless it proves fraud | Another reason to keep renewing and not switch casually |
| **Medical loading** | Extra premium charged because of a condition | Usually continues at every renewal. Ask for it per condition |
| **Permanent exclusion** | A declared condition the insurer will never cover (only with your consent, under current rules) | Acceptable for a minor issue; a deal-breaker for a major one |
| **Underwriting** | The insurer's medical review of your proposal | Its written result is what you should compare |
| **Co-pay** | The % of every claim you pay yourself | Watch for age-based, zone-based or hospital-based co-pay hidden in the fine print |
| **Deductible** | A fixed amount you pay before the policy pays | Useful only as a deliberate "top-up" design |
| **Room-rent limit** | A cap on the room category or rent per day | Exceeding it cuts **the whole bill** proportionately (see file 04) |
| **Consumables / non-medical items** | Gloves, PPE kits, syringes and similar; IRDAI keeps a list of items | Can be 5–15% of a bill; often an add-on |
| **Restoration / recharge** | Refills the sum insured after it's used in a year | Check if it works for the same illness and the same person |
| **No-claim bonus (NCB / cumulative bonus)** | Sum insured grows each claim-free year | Check how much is lost after a claim |
| **Pre/post-hospitalisation** | Tests and medicines before admission and after discharge, e.g. 60/180 days | Matters a lot for chronic conditions |
| **Day-care procedure** | Treatment needing under 24 h admission (cataract, chemo…) | Should be covered without a 24 h stay |
| **Domiciliary treatment** | Treatment at home when hospital admission isn't possible | Often excludes diabetes, BP and arthritis |
| **AYUSH** | Ayurveda, yoga, Unani, Siddha, homeopathy treatment | Usually covered up to SI in recognised hospitals |
| **Zone pricing** | Premium depends on the city (metros cost more) | Treatment in a higher zone may add a co-pay |
| **Network hospital** | Hospital with a cashless tie-up with the insurer | Lists change; confirm at the hospital |
| **Excluded hospital** | A hospital the insurer won't pay at all, even by reimbursement | Check this list too, not just the network list |
| **Cashless** | Insurer pays the hospital directly | Needs pre-authorisation |
| **Reimbursement** | You pay, then claim with bills | Needs original documents within a deadline (often 15–30 days) |
| **TPA (third-party administrator)** | A company that processes claims for the insurer | Adds a layer; ask who actually handles your claims |
| **Portability** | Moving to another insurer while keeping credit for waiting periods already served | Credit applies only up to the old sum insured |
| **Migration** | Moving to another plan of the **same** insurer with credit | Different from portability |
| **Free-look period** | Days after buying when you can cancel for a refund (30 days under current rules) | Use it to read the actual policy you were issued |
| **Grace period** | Time to pay a late renewal without losing benefits (often 30 days) | Some policies give **no cover during grace**. Renew early |
| **UIN** | Unique ID IRDAI gives each product | Use it to fetch the exact wording |
| **CIS** | Customer Information Sheet, a short summary | Handy, but the policy wording wins if they differ |
| **Prospectus vs policy wording** | Prospectus = sales and price document; wording = the contract | Rate tables are in the prospectus; rules are in the wording |
| **Claim settlement ratio (CSR)** | % of claims settled by number | Easily flattered; see file 07 |
| **Incurred claim ratio (ICR)** | Claims paid ÷ premium earned | Very low = stingy, very high = may raise prices |
| **Complaints per 10,000 claims** | How often customers complain to the regulator | The best single signal of claim behaviour |
| **Section 80D** | Income-tax deduction for health premiums (old tax regime) | ₹25k for self/family, ₹25k for parents (₹50k if a parent is 60+) |
