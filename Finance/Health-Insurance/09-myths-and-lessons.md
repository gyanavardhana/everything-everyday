# Myths and Lessons

| What we heard / believed | What turned out to be true |
|---|---|
| "Tell the agent and they'll handle disclosure" | Only the signed proposal form counts. Undisclosed conditions are the most common reason claims are rejected |
| "HbA1c above 7 means automatic rejection" | No insurer publishes a fixed cut-off. Your actual value decides loading or acceptance |
| "This plan gives extra benefits at special partner hospitals" | Check the policy wording. If a benefit isn't in the documents, it doesn't exist. Ask for it in writing |
| "A chronic-disease rider covers all my conditions early" | Riders cover only their **listed** conditions (often diabetes, BP, asthma, cholesterol). Thyroid, joints and others stay on normal waits |
| "A high claim settlement ratio means a good insurer" | Partial payments count as settled. Complaints per 10k claims tells you more |
| "The aggregator's hospital list is current" | Lists can be months old and differ between sites. Use the insurer's dated list and call the hospital |
| "A brochure price is what I'll pay" | Loading for conditions can add a lot. Compare final written quotes |
| "AI chatbot summaries of a policy are accurate" | They mixed up ages, plan versions and add-ons. Always check against the official PDF, with page numbers |
| "Pay for every wait reduction available" | Only worth it if nothing else covers the gap. Work out the one-time cost against the months saved |
| "Brochure and policy wording say the same thing" | The CIS or brochure can leave out exceptions. The wording is the contract |
| "Monthly EMI is just a payment convenience" | It costs extra, removes long-term discounts, and unpaid instalments may be deducted from a claim |

## Process lessons
- Keep a **dated** folder of every official PDF you rely on (prospectus, wording, CIS, rate chart, form).
- Write questions with page references so the insurer can't give vague answers.
- Decide the decision rule before the quotes arrive.
- Notice your own bias (e.g. knowing an agent personally) and check against data.
